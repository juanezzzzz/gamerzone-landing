# GAMERZONE — DESIGN BRIEF

## Positioning

**Premium gaming club.**

Not an internet cafe.
Not a gaming store.
Not an esports website.
Not a cyberpunk theme park.

GamerZone is a **physical entertainment destination** in Yopal, Colombia. Four curated experiences: competitive PC gaming, social console sessions, football on big screens, and a private VIP room.

The digital presence must match the ambition of the physical space.

---

## Brand Feel

| Word | What It Means Here |
|---|---|
| **Premium** | Quality of craft, not price signaling. Think Apple Store, not "luxury" gold borders |
| **Cinematic** | The scroll feels like watching a film. Scenes, transitions, pacing |
| **Social** | Gaming here is a shared experience. Friends, couples, groups |
| **Technical** | The hardware matters. Specs are a selling point, presented with authority |
| **Confident** | The design doesn't try too hard. Restraint. Negative space. Let the content breathe |
| **Immersive** | You should feel like you're already in the space before you visit |

## Visual Identity

### Photography Direction
- **Priority 1**: Real photos from the business (already extracted as stills from their videos)
- **Priority 2**: Enhanced versions via Magnific (upscaled, color-graded)
- **Priority 3**: Complementary stock from Pixabay (matched to the real photos' look)
- **Never**: Generic stock that could be any gaming cafe anywhere

Photos should show **the experience** (people playing, the atmosphere, the social moment) more than **the equipment** (just RGB towers and monitors).

### Color Language
- **Dark = premium**: `#080808` through `#222222` — the space is a dark room with colored lighting
- **Blue = action**: `#2563EB` — every CTA, every interactive element, every "do this now" moment
- **Gold = VIP only**: `#D4AF37` — reserved exclusively for the VIP section. Using it anywhere else dilutes it
- **Photography = the color**: The images bring the warmth, the RGB, the life. The UI stays dark and restrained around them

### Typographic Voice
- **Sora** is the brand voice. Uppercase, tight tracking, massive when it needs to be. It IS the composition in statement sections
- **DM Sans** is the conversational voice. Friendly, readable, never the star
- **JetBrains Mono** is the technical voice. Specs, data, labels, metadata. It says "we take the hardware seriously"

## What Premium Looks Like Here

### It IS:
- Negative space that lets content breathe
- Photography that fills the viewport and sets the mood
- Typography that acts as visual composition, not just labeling
- Smooth, purposeful motion that guides the eye
- A scroll experience that feels like a curated tour
- Specs presented with the confidence of a car configurator
- A VIP section that feels genuinely exclusive (different surface, gold accents, more space)

### It is NOT:
- Gold everywhere
- Excessive gradients
- Glassmorphism and frosted panels
- Neon glow effects
- "Gaming" cliches (glitch text, pixel fonts, controller icons)
- Dashboard aesthetics (data grids, rounded stat cards)
- Template layouts (equal cards in a grid)
- Every section looking like every other section with different text

## Visual References (Principles, Not Copies)

The full reference board is in `REFERENCES/REFERENCES.md` with screenshots.

| Reference | What to Extract |
|---|---|
| **Apple product pages** | Massive typography, product as protagonist, alternating layouts per feature, restraint |
| **Aman Hotels** | Photography dominance, whitespace as luxury, each property has its own identity |
| **Tesla/Porsche** | Specs as confident badges, split asymmetric layouts, scroll-driven reveals |
| **Stripe** | Typographic hierarchy, mono as a system, gradient type for dimension |
| **Locomotive** | Typography that IS the composition, viewport-width headings |
| **Equinox** | Lifestyle > service, dark theme, aspirational photography |

**Rule**: Extract principles from these references. Never copy layouts.

## Audience

The people who come to GamerZone:
- **Groups of friends** (most common) — 3-6 people, weekends, wanting to play together
- **Couples** — console sessions, casual, social
- **Competitive gamers** — PC arena, they care about specs and latency
- **Birthday/event groups** — VIP room, private, special occasion
- **Solo players** — PC session, experienced, know what they want

The website speaks to them as **peers who take gaming seriously**, not as customers who need to be sold. The tone is confident and direct, never corporate or salesy.

---

## Competitive Context

In Yopal, there is no comparable digital presence for a gaming venue. The competitive set isn't local — it's the user's general expectation of what a premium website looks like, informed by Apple, Nike, Netflix, and the sites they visit daily.

That is the bar. Not "good for a gaming cafe."
